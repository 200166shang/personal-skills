# Faithful illustrated lecture scripts

## Default: preserve the lecture

Keep the speaker's original progression, first/second-person voice, explanations, reasoning, examples, comparisons, conditions, uncertainty, recommendations, course-scope remarks, numbers and conclusions. Retain substantive restatements that emphasize an idea or add a reason or qualification. Preserve small operational details such as which button was pressed, what appeared next, why the speaker repeated a setting and how success was recognized. Read and account for the complete source, not selected highlights.

Clean speech into sentences and natural paragraphs. Remove only pauses, filler sounds, accidental stutters, abandoned word fragments and immediate duplicates without added meaning. Keep rhetorical questions and transitions when they carry the line of thought. Correct clear ASR/verbal slips using surrounding speech, repeated terms, actual UI text or targeted playback. Preserve substantive claims even if they seem technically mistaken; report unresolved recognition or factual questions separately rather than silently rewriting the lesson.

Use light headings where the speaker actually changes topic. Keep enumerations when the speaker enumerates; otherwise prefer continuous paragraphs. Screenshots support the adjacent speech. Formatting does not authorize conversion into a checklist, comparison table, key-point summary, third-person report or a newly structured lesson. Avoid invented introductions, explanations, examples and closing summaries. Describe the result as a cleaned faithful script, not a verbatim transcript or guaranteed exact record of the audio.

For each window, compare every source segment to the draft, then check every draft claim against the source. Review the merged script in source order as well; merging should not rewrite reviewed paragraphs. Character counts are warning signals only: a ratio below 70% calls for another complete comparison and an explanation of the reduction. A higher ratio does not establish fidelity. Never add filler to satisfy a ratio.

If translating into a requested language, preserve the same substance, order and speaker voice. Identify the result as a translation, preserving the original transcript for reference.

## Explicit summary requests

Use summary mode only when the user asks to summarize, condense or extract key points. Then group nearby repetition and organize around useful headings while preserving important explanations, steps, examples and qualifiers. An ordinary request for notes, HTML or good readability remains faithful mode. Keep prior summaries as separate editions when generating faithful replacements.

## Figures and source evidence

Choose a figure when it carries information the text benefits from: a wiring relationship, complete dialog, relevant option, code change or verification result. Avoid repeated title screens and transitional or obstructed frames. Check the actual single frame; contact sheets are previews, not final figures. Captions are separate editorial descriptions of what is visible, not additions placed in the speaker's voice.

Use original source times and part numbers in links. Source resolution limits screenshot detail; enlarging a low-resolution frame does not restore lost text. Keep the complete speech even when a useful screenshot is unavailable, and report the visual limitation separately.
