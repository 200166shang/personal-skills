# Readable tutorial notes

Organize around the speaker's teaching progression. Combine nearby repetition, remove greetings and filler, and keep the explanation behind the important actions. A useful step states its prerequisite, action and expected result when the source supplies them.

Use specific section headings. Choose paragraphs for reasoning, lists for steps, and tables for real comparisons or mappings. Preserve meaningful examples, conditions, warnings made by the speaker, paths, commands, versions and parameters. Resolve obvious ASR errors through actual UI text, repeated terminology or targeted playback. Keep substantive uncertainty visible in the delivery report.

Explain from the supplied transcript and viewed frames. Do not invent missing menu labels, download URLs, code, version numbers or results. Distinguish the tutorial's historical demonstration from claims about current software. No fixed summary ratio, mandatory image count or per-section template is needed.

Choose a figure when it carries information the text benefits from: a wiring relationship, a complete dialog, a relevant option, a code change or a verification result. Avoid repeated title screens and transitional or obstructed frames. Check the original single frame after preview selection; do not use a contact sheet as the final figure. A caption says what is actually visible and connects it to the adjacent explanation.

Final captions and time links support revisiting the source. Use the source part number when generating links. Source-media resolution limits screenshot detail; enlarging a low-resolution frame does not restore lost text. Prefer a clear frame and honest description to an unreadable image labelled as proof.
